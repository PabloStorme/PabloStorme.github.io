about

I took the long way into this, and I'd do it again.

I spent two years on Biochemistry and Biotechnology in Antwerp and Ghent before admitting the obvious: I wanted to build things, not pipette them. I finished a Bachelor in Applied Computer Science at HoGent and have been shipping software ever since.

These days I work at Human8, where most of my work sits where messy input meets a system that has to make sense of it — document processing, search, LLM and agent tooling, and the developer platform around all of it. I like the unglamorous parts: the pipeline that stops silently failing, the internal tool that saves the team an afternoon a week.

Outside work I build for myself, mostly in the open. Odysseus is the largest of those — a self-hosted AI workspace I run on my own hardware because I wanted to know exactly how the whole stack fits together.

Odysseus

personal · Python · full stack

A self-hosted AI workspace — the ChatGPT-style experience, running entirely on your own hardware and your own data. Chat against local or hosted models, an agent that picks up tools and finishes the task, hardware-aware model recommendations, multi-step research runs, persistent memory and skills, plus mail, notes and calendar the agent can actually act on.

Human8 · internal platform

A shared plugin marketplace that puts the same AI workflows in front of the dev, product and QA teams — packaged skills for planning, review and debugging, on top of an Azure DevOps MCP server. One install, and the whole team's tooling agrees with itself.

Document processing platform

Human8 · backend & observability

The pipeline in the hero, in production: research documents in, chunked, embedded, summarised and served back as something teams can search and reason over. I also built the tooling to trace a single file through every step of it, which is how you find out where processing quietly stalled.

beyond code

Five years in the scouts, two of them leading the group.

Group leader is an unpaid management job with a volunteer team, a budget, other people's children and no authority to fall back on. It is the best training I've had for anything I do at work.

The rest of it: teaching Scratch to eight-year-olds each summer, fixing laptops for neighbours who'd otherwise give up on them, and a stint at Beego. I keep ending up somewhere between building the thing and helping someone else use it.
